# Classifying image input representations from evidence

## Summary and question addressed

This topic asks what can be established about image data from a file's structure and metadata, without treating a filename extension or a user-facing “RAW” label as proof of its pixel representation. The proposed description keeps container, resource role, sample layout, signal encoding, demosaic state, processing history and temporal composition separate. Adobe DNG can describe CFA or LinearRaw resources; Apple ProRAW is a linearized, already-demosaiced DNG; Apple ProRes RAW carries Bayer-pattern video samples for later demosaicing. [V] The format definitions for JPEG/JFIF, PNG and H.264 add examples of coded image components and their metadata. [V] They do not establish how a camera produced the source image. [I] The taxonomy and its confidence policy are research conventions, not an industry standard. [D] Coverage is selective and source-based; it is not a decoder specification, complete format matrix or proof of any camera's capture pipeline.

## Evidence and scope

- [V] DNG 1.7.1.0 is a TIFF 6.0-based container extension. Its IFDs identify sample interpretation and resource roles; the filename suffix alone does not say which IFD or representation is being inspected.
- [V] In DNG, `PhotometricInterpretation` 32803 identifies CFA data; `PhotometricInterpretation` 34892 identifies LinearRaw. LinearRaw is used for non-CFA cameras or data already demosaiced from a CFA. An enhanced image resource is at least demosaiced. A file can include full-resolution image data, reduced images, previews and enhanced data.
- [V] DNG requires the `DNGVersion` tag (50706) in IFD 0, while readers should accept either a `.DNG` or `.TIF` extension. [I] A valid `DNGVersion` is therefore stronger evidence of DNG than the extension.
- [V] CFA data (`PhotometricInterpretation` 32803) requires the `CFARepeatPatternDim` and `CFAPattern` tags in the same IFD.
- [V] `NewSubFileType` also marks non-primary resources: 4/5 for transparency masks, 8/9 for depth maps and 65540 (10004.H) for semantic masks. Apple states that ProRAW files can store semantic masks, such as people, skin and sky (Apple does not state which tag stores them).
- [V] Apple describes ProRAW as DNG-based and linearized; its image is already demosaiced and can incorporate multi-frame computational processing. “RAW” therefore does not imply a mosaic at the final stored image resource. Apple also states that the stored samples are, by default, 12-bit values written through an adaptive companding curve, which the DNG `LinearizationTable` maps back to linear scene values.
- [V] Apple ProRes RAW encodes Bayer-pattern video samples and defers demosaicing to playback. Apple contrasts this with conventional video, which is demosaiced and processed in camera into viewable RGB images before recording. [V] In that white paper, RGB means three primary values at each pixel: “conventional RGB images consist of not one but three primary color values (red, green, and blue) at each pixel location” (SRC-PB003-04, Apple ProRes RAW white paper, May 2023, printed p. 4). [I] The recorded codec samples of such video may still be luma/chroma arrays, as in H.264 (CLM-PB005-05).
- [I] A useful classifier reports evidence field-by-field instead of forcing a single “RAW/rendered” label. Where fields conflict, are absent or identify different resources, retain `unknown` or `mixed` and the reason.
- [D] Confidence grades below are a proposed operational policy: **high** = the resource structure is validated and the field is interpreted using an applicable, versioned definition; **medium** = an applicable vendor/format declaration or typed metadata exists but the payload resource has not been validated; **low** = extension, UI label or filename only; **unknown** = absent, contradictory or out-of-scope evidence. Confidence applies per field, not to the file as a whole. A format-level vendor statement supports that format's documented design, not an uninspected file's actual payload.
- [U] No claim is made here about the exact payload of any Panasonic LUMIX mode, HEIF/HEIC, TIFF, camera RAW family beyond the documented DNG examples, or video codecs beyond the documented ProRes RAW and H.264 cases. Extensions and familiar product names do not settle those questions.

## Selected coded still-image and video representations

These examples describe coding and file syntax. They do not establish source provenance, camera processing history, or whether values in a particular file depict demosaiced scene color. A pixel grid can encode a sensor mosaic as arbitrary image samples; only an applicable format declaration or validated payload semantics establish that layout.

- **JPEG/JFIF:** ITU-T T.871 defines JFIF as an interchange format for images encoded with JPEG (T.81). A conforming JFIF image has one or three 8-bit colour channels. In three-channel JFIF, component order is Y, Cb, Cr under its specified YCbCr interpretation. The required JFIF APP0 marker follows SOI and carries fields such as version, pixel density/aspect information and optional thumbnail data. JPEG coding syntax and JFIF packaging are separate layers. [V]
- **PNG:** The PNG reference image is a rectangular pixel array; the PNG datastream represents grayscale, truecolor, indexed-color, grayscale-with-alpha or truecolor-with-alpha pixels. IHDR identifies bit depth and color type; samples may be 1–16 bits subject to type-specific combinations, while indexed-color pixels are palette indices. Image data uses the specified filtering and DEFLATE compression. Decoding reverses the PNG coding and specified sample transformations, but the format cannot prove that the encoder preserved its upstream source before forming the reference image. [V]
- **AVC/H.264:** The standard describes coded video pictures and decoded component arrays, not a camera file container. In the pinned 2024 edition, `chroma_format_idc` identifies monochrome, 4:2:0, 4:2:2 or 4:4:4 organization; 4:4:4 can also signal separately coded colour planes. Luma and chroma bit depths can be 8–14 and need not match. VUI metadata can signal colour range, primaries, transfer characteristics and matrix coefficients. When an optional field is absent, apply only the standard's specified inferred value; some colour-description defaults are explicitly unspecified/application-determined. Record whether a value was signalled or inferred. [V]
- **[I]** The stream's coded/decoded sample organization is stronger evidence about its digital representation than a filename suffix, but it does not reveal debayering, tone mapping, denoising or other operations before encoding. H.264 profiles and metadata are parameterized; do not assign one chroma format, bit depth or colour interpretation to every H.264 stream.
- **[U]** The current H.264 edition (V16, June 2026) is listed in force by ITU. This draft uses V15 (August 2024) for the technical clauses cited below; V16's full text was not reviewed for this update. Recheck the technical claims against V16 before implementation or conformance work.

## Definitions, units and prerequisites

Describe each identified resource with a tuple:

`(container, resource role, sample layout, signal encoding, demosaic state, processing history, temporal composition, evidence)`

- **Container**: the file-level organization (for example, DNG/TIFF, JPEG, HEIF/ISO-BMFF, or a video container). A container may wrap or index multiple resources.
- **Resource role**: primary image, reduced image, preview, alternate image, depth/auxiliary resource, or video track, where the format exposes that distinction.
- **Sample layout**: CFA mosaic, per-pixel RGB/RGBA, luma/chroma planes, or unknown. State subsampling and bit depth only when directly available and relevant.
- **Signal encoding**: integer or floating-point representation and transfer/photometric interpretation when known; do not infer a transfer function from a “log”, “RAW” or codec label alone.
- **Demosaic state**: mosaiced, demosaiced, or unknown for the specific resource. CFA tags and an explicit format definition can support this field; a container extension cannot.
- **Processing history**: known vendor/computational fusion, rendered, minimally documented, or unknown. Separate the file's structural evidence from a vendor's capture-pipeline description.
- **Temporal composition**: single frame, sequence/video, multi-frame fused still, or unknown. A single output still may have been computationally combined from multiple exposures.
- **Evidence**: source, version, exact tag/box/section or timestamp, resource identifier, observed value, and per-field confidence.

No physical units are introduced by this taxonomy. If a downstream topic interprets sample values as radiance, exposure, color coordinates or time, it must define units and assumptions there.

## Mathematical foundations and derivations

Not applicable. This is an evidence and representation classification, not a pixel transform.

## Parameters, ranges, estimation and uncertainty

The classifier records observed values rather than estimating missing ones. It must preserve unknowns rather than filling them from common conventions. For example, a video codec profile may establish coded sample layout and bit depth while leaving the camera's earlier debayer, denoising, sharpening, or tone operations undocumented. Confidence is assigned to each tuple field and is not a probability of correctness.

## Reference algorithm and pseudocode

This is a generic research contract, not a production decoder recommendation:

1. Identify the container using validated structure; retain the filename extension as a low-confidence hint.
2. Enumerate image resources/tracks exposed by that container. Do not merge preview, primary, enhanced and auxiliary resources into one classification.
3. Read the format-defined fields that describe resource role, sample organization, photometric interpretation, encoding and dimensions. Record the source/version of each definition and the observed value.
4. Add vendor capture-pipeline statements as separate processing-history evidence. Do not treat them as observed payload properties unless the payload is independently validated.
5. Assign confidence independently to every field. If evidence conflicts, mark the affected field `mixed` or `unknown`, retain both observations and identify the unresolved question.
6. Return all resource tuples and evidence references. Never silently promote an extension-based guess into a verified payload classification.

## Alternative methods and comparative evidence

Extension-only classification is cheap but cannot distinguish a DNG CFA IFD from a LinearRaw IFD, or the main image from an embedded preview. A vendor “RAW” label is useful workflow metadata but may cover linearized or computationally processed data. Structural inspection is stronger for the encoded resource but does not, by itself, reveal every prior operation in the camera pipeline. A vendor document can establish intended behavior for a named format/mode, while actual payload validation is needed to confirm that a particular file conforms and which resource is being inspected. These methods answer different questions and should be recorded as separate evidence.

## Edge cases, failure modes and safeguards

- **Multiple resources in one file:** DNG may contain multiple IFDs and preview/enhanced resources. Classify each separately; the primary-image result must not be copied onto every IFD.
- **Linearized and computational RAW:** DNG LinearRaw and ProRAW are not CFA mosaics at the stored image resource. Keep sensor origin, mosaic state and processing history distinct.
- **Unknown vendor metadata:** do not treat undocumented values as standardized. Preserve the raw tag/box identifier and mark interpretation unknown.
- **Video pipeline ambiguity:** encoded RGB or Y′CbCr samples do not prove that earlier camera processing was absent. ProRes RAW's Bayer organization is a documented exception; do not generalize it to other codecs.
- **Malformed, partial or transcoded files:** a recognized container signature does not guarantee complete or conforming content. Report parse/validation status separately from the format's nominal properties.
- **Evidence disagreement:** record both the observed payload and vendor statement; lower confidence for the disputed field until the exact mode/version and resource can be reconciled.

## Panasonic LUMIX-specific notes

Unknown. This research does not inspect private media or claim the sample organization, encoding or processing history of any LUMIX RAW/video mode. Establish mode-specific behavior only from an exact public format definition or an authorized, reproducible public sample with payload inspection.

## Video/cinema extension

The same tuple applies per video track and, where relevant, per frame or auxiliary stream. Record codec/profile, coded sample organization, bit depth, chroma subsampling, transfer/primaries/matrix metadata and whether demosaicing is deferred only when supported by the stream or an applicable format definition. Do not infer camera-side processing history from the delivery codec alone. ProRes RAW is the covered documented example; broader codec and camera-mode coverage remains open.

## Implementation notes for software

No app-specific behavior is recommended here. A future implementation should keep parsing, evidence capture and product policy separable, and should expose unknown/mixed states rather than presenting a guessed single label as fact. Any decoder choice, supported-format list, trust policy or UI wording belongs in the Perception Editor repository and requires its own acceptance.
