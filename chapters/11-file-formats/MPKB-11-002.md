# Panasonic RW2 structure and what original DC-S5 files record

## Summary and question addressed

What does a Panasonic RW2 file contain, and which of its properties are documented by Panasonic rather than reverse-engineered or seen only in a few DC-S5 files? Panasonic's S5 manual says RAW pictures use the `.RW2` extension and are always recorded at the large 3:2 size. [V]/A. Whether Panasonic documents the S5's still-RAW bit depth or compression anywhere is [U]: a full-text search of one manual edition (DVQP2213ZD) and the spec page found no statement, and no Panasonic RW2 format specification was found (CLM-PB007-02). The container details come from ExifTool and LibRaw, which are reverse-engineered, and from three owner-provided S5 samples whose measurements cannot be re-run. [I] In those samples an RW2 is a single-IFD, TIFF-like file. Its header has 0x0055 where TIFF has 42, followed by a 16-byte signature. Read with ExifTool's and LibRaw's reverse-engineered tag meanings, it holds one 14-bit CFA plane in a fixed-size packed encoding (`RawFormat` 6), per-channel black levels, linearity limits, a 1920×1280 JPEG preview and a vendor-private distortion block. [I]/C, owner-reported (see "Evidence and scope"). A fixed-size encoding like this cannot be lossless for every input. [I]/C. Decoding the samples showed RGGB at the crop origin and, under LibRaw's reverse-engineered block layout, showed that about 7.5–11.7% of pixels (denominator convention unrecorded) are stored with a coarser step of 2, 4 or 16 DN. [I]/C, owner-reported. Measuring the size of the loss needs a lossless reference. [U] Research draft; S5 modes missing from the samples are not covered.

## Evidence and scope

Evidence comes in three kinds, and the labels keep them apart:

1. **Manufacturer documents** (SRC-PB007-01–04). The blueprint's source hierarchy (§1.2) puts vendor technical manuals in the normative tier, and grade A is a directly consulted primary norm or specification, so a claim limited to what Panasonic itself states about its own camera (file extension, picture size, menu defaults, firmware history) is [V]/A. Earlier drafts graded these B, but B means reviewed research, which they are not. They are operating manuals and support pages, not a format specification, so they support no container or encoding detail they do not state.
2. **Reverse-engineered implementations** (SRC-PB007-06 ExifTool, SRC-PB007-07 LibRaw, SRC-PB007-08 libopenraw). These record what a decoder does. A format fact drawn from them is [I] at grade C, or D for an unexplained secondary statement. Panasonic has not confirmed any of it.
3. **Owner-provided Lumix S5 sample files, not redistributed** (SRC-PB007-09). Three files, inspected read-only on 2026-10-21. Tools: a purpose-written Python 3.14.7 header and IFD reader; ExifTool 13.55, installed with Homebrew (SRC-PB007-10); and rawpy 0.27.1 with its bundled LibRaw 0.22.1, plus a numpy transcription of LibRaw's RawFormat 6 decoder (`decoders_libraw.cpp` lines 382–535) used as a transcription check, all in a temporary virtual environment under `/tmp` (SRC-PB007-11). Collin authorized metadata reads, a second-tool check and pixel-level decoding for this task. Only allowlisted format fields and aggregate statistics were printed, and nothing derived from the pixels was saved. **Label for these results: [I]/C, owner-reported.** Measured on 3 owner-provided samples; inputs not redistributable; scripts not retained. CONTRIBUTING requires reproducible input, settings and results for an experiment, and no reviewer can re-derive these, so they are not [V] (relabelled from [V]/C in 0.3.0). Per-file values that could single out a file (exact preview byte lengths, Gamma, distortion values, black levels, orientation, burst flag and per-sample pixel statistics) are given only as ranges across the samples or as 'within X' statements, and no value is tied to an individual sample.

Standards used for contrast: TIFF 6.0 (SRC-PB007-05) and DNG 1.7.1.0 (SRC-PB003-01).

Out of scope or deferred: pixel decoding beyond the three checks in "Sample cross-check and pixel-level results", colour matrices and colour rendering, S5 noise and dynamic range (blueprint 08.03), High Resolution mode RW2, burst and 6K/4K photo RAW, other Panasonic bodies (the S5II is a different camera), RWL and older `.RAW` files, and any parser or application recommendation. The firmware-specific S5 capability record belongs to blueprint chapter 08 (`08.02`). This module is the format-level "native RW2 metadata audit" listed for chapter 11.

## Definitions, units and prerequisites

- **IFD**: TIFF image file directory, a counted list of 12-byte entries (tag, type, count, value or offset). TIFF 6.0 §2.
- **CFA**: colour filter array. The 2×2 repeat is written row by row, so RGGB means the top row is red, green and the bottom row is green, blue.
- **DN**: digital number, the stored integer sample value. Black levels and linearity limits are in DN at the stated bit depth.
- **Sensor area and crop**: `SensorWidth`×`SensorHeight` is the stored raster. The `Sensor*Border` and `Crop*` tags give a rectangle inside it.
- **RawFormat**: Panasonic tag 0x002d (`pana_encoding` in LibRaw). Its value meanings are reverse-engineered.
- Prerequisite: [MPKB-11-001](MPKB-11-001.md) for the per-resource taxonomy (container, resource role, sample layout, encoding, demosaic state).

## Mathematical foundations and derivations

**Header.** In TIFF 6.0, bytes 0–1 give the byte order ("II" or "MM"), bytes 2–3 hold 42, and bytes 4–7 hold the offset of the first IFD (SRC-PB007-05 §2, p. 13). In all three samples the first eight bytes are `49 49 55 00 18 00 00 00`: little-endian, identifier 0x0055, IFD0 at offset 24. Bytes 8–23 equal the 16-byte value that ExifTool checks for (`88 e7 74 d8 f8 25 1d 4d 94 7a 6e 77 82 2b 5d 6a`). [I]/C, owner-reported. A strict TIFF 6.0 reader that requires 42 will reject the file. [I]/C

**Row packing (RawFormat 6, 14-bit).** In every sample, `StripByteCounts` = 35,469,312 bytes and `SensorHeight` = 4016 rows. Each row is therefore 8832 bytes, or 552 blocks of 16 bytes. LibRaw 0.22.1's `panasonicC6_load_raw` decodes 11 samples per 16-byte block when BitsPerSample is 14 and 14 per block when it is 12 (`decoders_libraw.cpp` lines 455–456). 552 × 11 = 6072 = `SensorWidth`. The arithmetic is exact, but the values it uses are owner-reported, so it is [I]/C, owner-reported. The block interpretation comes from LibRaw and is [I]/C.

**Why it cannot be lossless for every input.** A 16-byte block has 2^128 possible states, and eleven independent 14-bit samples have 2^154 possible combinations. A fixed-size block cannot give every combination its own encoding, so some distinct inputs must share one. That follows from the fixed block size alone, and the byte count is the same in all three samples. On average each sample gets 128/11 ≈ 11.64 stored bits. [I]/C. How large the loss is, and where it falls, is [U]. LibRaw's decoder reads a 2-bit selector for each group of three samples and multiplies by 1, 2, 4 or 16. That points to a variable quantization step, measured in "Sample cross-check and pixel-level results", but the encoder's rule is inferred from decoder code. Panasonic has not described it. [I]/C

## Sample cross-check and pixel-level results

These checks were run on 2026-10-21 on the same three samples. Collin authorized a second-tool check and read-only pixel decoding. Everything below is [I]/C, owner-reported, for these three files only: measured on 3 owner-provided samples; inputs not redistributable; scripts not retained. Anything wider is labelled separately.

**Second-tool cross-check (ExifTool 13.55, Homebrew).** ExifTool calls all three files `RW2`, which means its signature rule matched. It agrees with the Python reader on every field compared: Make/Model, PanasonicRawVersion `0400`, the 6072×4016 sensor size, borders and crop 8/12/4008/6012, SamplesPerPixel 1, CFAPattern `[Red,Green][Green,Blue]`, BitsPerSample 14, Compression 34316 ("Panasonic RAW 1"), RawFormat 6, black levels 509–510 DN per channel across samples, linearity limits 16383, StripByteCounts 35,469,312, Multishot 0, Orientation (varied, 1 or 8; per-file values withheld), Gamma (about 500–525, shown ÷256 as about 1.96–2.05; per-file values withheld), preview byte lengths (about 0.6–0.7 MB each; exact values withheld) at 1920×1280, DistortionCorrection On, FirmwareVersion `0 2 6 0` and DiffractionCorrection Off. Neither tool reports anything the other contradicts. ExifTool's `[IFD1]` group in its output is the embedded JPEG's own EXIF, not a second IFD in the RW2 chain. ExifTool also decodes the distortion block: DistortionScale is about 1.015–1.018 in all three, and the other parameters also vary between files (per-file values withheld). That variation is owner-reported. What the parameters mean is [I]/D. The version installed (13.55, Homebrew bottle) is older than the 13.59 source cited in SRC-PB007-06. Compared at source level on GitHub, 13.55 (commit `de11d24`, "Update to 13.55") and 13.59 (`2200871`) have a byte-identical `PanasonicRaw.pm`, an identical `DoProcessTIFF` in `ExifTool.pm` (which holds the RW2 signature block), and a `Panasonic.pm` that differs only by one line added at 13.59 line 364 (`'16 225'=> '225-area 2'`, an `AFAreaMode` print value, a tag not read here). So the version difference does not affect these readings. [V]/C for the source comparison; it assumes the Homebrew bottle `13.55_1` was built from the 13.55 release.

**(a) CFA layout.** Method: rawpy 0.27.1 (LibRaw 0.22.1) decoded the mosaic, and the 6000×4000 crop was split by 2×2 position (row, column) relative to the crop origin. Three signals point the same way. Only the first is independent of Panasonic's own processing (see the paragraph after the list):

1. Positions (0,1) and (1,0) have almost equal means: in every sample they are within 0.1% (at most about 4 DN) of each other, at roughly 6,000–6,400 DN above black across samples. In every sample they are also the most strongly correlated pair (r = 0.996–0.998 across samples, against 0.933–0.983 for (0,0)/(1,1)). That is the expected signature of the two green sites.
2. The colour ratio at (0,0) relative to green, averaged over 5×5 superpixel blocks, rises and falls with the embedded preview's R/G ratio (r = 0.31–0.81 across samples) and not with its B/G ratio (|r| ≤ 0.25). The ratio at (1,1) follows B/G (r = 0.62–0.69) and not R/G (r between −0.38 and −0.02). In every sample, each matching correlation exceeds the mismatched one by at least 0.3.
3. On blocks the preview renders as near-neutral (roughly 1,300–6,700 blocks per file), the median (0,0)/G is 0.45–0.48 across samples and the median (1,1)/G is 0.55–0.57. In every sample, the (0,0)/G median is within 0.025 of the reciprocal of the as-shot red WB multiplier and the (1,1)/G median within 0.01 of the reciprocal of the blue multiplier, while each is at least 0.07 away from the reciprocal of the other multiplier. If red and blue were swapped, those pairs would not match.

LibRaw reports `raw_pattern` R,G/G,B (`RGBG`) from the full-raster origin. With both crop offsets even, that is the same phase. Result: the crop origin is RGGB in all three samples. [I]/C, owner-reported. Confidence is high for these files, because the tag, the green-pair statistics, the preview chroma and the as-shot white balance all agree. The preview is the camera's own rendering, and the WB multipliers come from the file's metadata, so neither is fully independent of Panasonic's processing. That every original-S5 RW2 has this phase is [I]/C.

**(b) RawFormat 6 quantization.** Method: a numpy transcription of the 16-byte block layout in LibRaw's `pana_cs6_page_decoder::read_page` and `panasonicC6_load_raw` (SRC-PB007-07, `decoders_libraw.cpp` lines 382–535 at 0.22.1). It was written for this check, kept outside the repository and not retained. Each block holds two 14-bit start values, one for each CFA colour in the row, and three groups. Each group is a 2-bit step selector followed by three 10-bit codes. That is 124 of the block's 128 bits. Selectors 0, 1, 2 and 3 mean steps of 1, 2, 4 and 16 DN. With steps 1, 2 and 4, a code is relative to the previous value of the same colour when that value is above the step's base. With step 16 it is absolute. The decoder then subtracts 15 from every value (line 523). A result up to 0xFFFF is stored unchanged (lines 524–525). Anything else saturates (lines 526–529): a negative result, which wraps in unsigned arithmetic, becomes 0, and a result above 0xFFFF becomes 16383 (`pixel_mask` 0x3fff for 14-bit, line 462). The transcription reproduced rawpy's decode exactly: 0 mismatching pixels in each file. [I]/C, owner-reported. This is a transcription check: it shows the transcription matches LibRaw, not that the layout is right. Both follow the same reverse-engineered model, so it does not confirm Panasonic's intent. [I]/C

Observed step usage on the visible crop:

| Measure (visible crop) | Range across the three samples |
| --- | --- |
| step 1 | 88.3–92.5% |
| step 2 | 3.3–5.1% |
| step 4 | 2.3–3.4% |
| step 16 | 1.9–3.4% |
| step > 1 (100% − step 1) | 7.5–11.7% |
| step > 1 below 1024 DN | 0.3–1.1% |
| step > 1 at 8192 DN or above | 10.0–22.1% |
| pixels ≥ 16319 DN | 8.6–26.3% |

[I]/C, owner-reported. Each range is taken row by row across the three samples, so the ends of different rows need not come from the same sample; per-sample values are withheld (privacy, 0.3.1). The "step > 1" row is 100% minus the step-1 share; it was added in 0.3.0 from the per-sample numbers. Each percentage was rounded to 0.1% on its own, so in one sample steps 2 + 4 + 16 add to 0.1 percentage point less than 100% − step 1; in the other two they agree. The text uses 100% − step 1. How each block's two 14-bit start values were counted is not recorded: they carry no selector and are stored at full precision, so they were either counted as step 1 or left out of the denominator, and the scripts were not retained. The denominator of every percentage here is therefore unrecorded. [U]. It matters, because they are 2 of every 11 stored pixels. If they were counted as step 1, the coarse-step share among the 9 selector-coded pixels of a block is about 11/9 of the figures above (about 9.2–14.3%; approximate, because the 6000-pixel crop is not a whole number of 11-pixel blocks).

Every step-16 pixel decodes to a value ≡ 1 (mod 16). That is guaranteed by the decoder's own arithmetic, not a finding about the camera: for selector 3 LibRaw multiplies the 10-bit code by 16 (`decoders_libraw.cpp` line 510), adds no previous value because the step-16 base of 8192 is not below 0x2000 (line 511), and subtracts 15 (line 523), so a nonzero code k decodes to 16k − 15. The observation only confirms that the transcription and LibRaw agree. Coarser steps are rarer in dark areas than in bright ones in all three files. Step use seems to follow how far a group's values spread from the previous same-colour value. The base window is 1024 codes × step. That suggests the selector depends on local contrast more than on brightness alone, but it is an inference from the decoder's arithmetic and was not tested against gradients. [I]/C

What can be concluded: in these files, about 7.5–11.7% of pixels (100% − step-1 share; the denominator is unrecorded, see the counting caveat above) sit on a grid of 2, 4 or 16 DN instead of 1 DN. Each such value could stand for any sensor value within one step, so the stored value is quantized. [I]/C: the counts are owner-reported, and the step map depends on the block layout, which CLM-PB007-23 labels [I]/C. What cannot be concluded: the actual error against the sensor's 14-bit output. That needs either a lossless reference capture of the same scene (none exists for the S5) or Panasonic's encoder rules (rounding or truncation, and how steps are chosen). [U]. A whole-image histogram does not show the loss. In every file, 8147 distinct code values were counted from 8192 DN upward with no gaps, because relative coding moves the grid from group to group. [I]/C, owner-reported. If that range is contiguous it ends at 8192 + 8147 − 1 = 16338 DN, and that upper end cannot be derived from LibRaw's code. The decoder subtracts 15 from every value (`decoders_libraw.cpp` line 523), so a block's 14-bit start value can reach 16383 − 15 = 16368 and a step-16 value 16 × 1023 − 15 = 16353. Relative codes add to the previous value, and the decoder stores any result up to 0xFFFF unchanged (lines 524–525); only a result above 0xFFFF saturates, to 16383 (lines 526–529, `pixel_mask` 0x3fff at line 462). So the decoder sets no 14-bit ceiling for results from 16384 to 65535. Whether 16338 was each file's maximum, a camera clip point or something else was not recorded, and the measurement cannot be re-run. [U]. Whether a 2–4 DN step is below photon shot noise at the signal levels where it is used is also [U]: sensor gain was not measured, and this is a conjecture, not a proposed improvement. Between 8.6% and 26.3% of crop pixels sit at or above LibRaw's working white level of 16319 (16383 − 64, `tiff.cpp` lines 164–166), so these samples are partly clipped and the statistics are not representative of a well-exposed scene. [I]/C, owner-reported

**(c) Vignetting compensation.** All three files carry Panasonic MakerNote tag 0x008a, which ExifTool names `ShadingCompensation`, with value 0 (Off), owner-reported. The manual's own term is [Vignetting Comp.], whose default is ON (p. 531). Reading 0x008a as that menu item is [I]/D: ExifTool gives the tag no notes. With all three files on the same setting there is no ON/OFF pair to compare. Whether the setting changes RW2 pixel data is still [U] and needs a new pair of flat-field shots. No comparison was attempted.

**Other mode metadata.** ExifTool reports `BurstMode` On in some samples and Off in others (per-sample values withheld). All three are still 14-bit, RawFormat 6, with identical strip sizes. [I]/C, owner-reported. So in these files, a burst flag does not come with a 12-bit RW2. ExifTool warns that BurstMode "decoding may be different for some models", and the samples do not record the burst speed setting, so this does not establish the S5's burst bit depth in general. [I]/D. All three report mechanical shutter and `ShootingMode` Manual. Electronic-shutter and high-speed modes are not covered.

**Visible area.** LibRaw 0.22.1 reports a visible area of 6024×4016 at margin 0. It separately exposes the Panasonic crop as 12/8/6000/4000. [I]/C, owner-reported. A consumer must say which rectangle it uses. [D]

## Reference algorithm and pseudocode

An outline for collecting evidence, consistent with MPKB-11-001. It is not a decoder.

1. Read 8 bytes. Require "II" or "MM". Record the 16-bit identifier as observed (0x2A for TIFF, 0x55 for Panasonic). Do not treat 0x55 as TIFF conformance.
2. If the identifier is 0x55 and the IFD0 offset is at least 0x18, compare bytes 8–23 with ExifTool's 16-byte signature and record whether they match. ExifTool classifies a mismatch as "RAW", meaning an older Panasonic file.
3. Parse IFD0, bounds-checking the entry count, value offsets and file size. Tags below 0x0100 are Panasonic-private in this IFD, so do not give them the EXIF or TIFF meanings of the same numbers. LibRaw switches to Panasonic interpretation when tag 0x0001 has count 4.
4. Record `BitsPerSample`, `Compression`, `RawFormat`, `SamplesPerPixel`, `CFAPattern`, sensor size, borders and crop. Check that `StripByteCounts` equals rows × (width / samples per block) × 16 for the claimed encoding. If it does not, mark the encoding unknown and do not guess.
5. Record black levels and linearity limits as observed values with their bit depth. Label any decoder adjustment (+15, −64) separately as a decoder convention.
6. Treat `JpgFromRaw` as its own rendered-preview resource (MPKB-11-001: the preview is not the primary image). Check its SOI and SOF markers and record its dimensions.
7. Keep `DistortionInfo` and other vendor blocks as opaque bytes, plus any reverse-engineered interpretation labelled with its source and version. Never apply them as verified corrections.

## Alternative methods and comparative evidence

**DNG as a contrast** (SRC-PB003-01, DNG 1.7.1.0). DNG keeps the TIFF magic number 42 and identifies itself with `DNGVersion`. Black and white levels are public tags (`BlackLevel` 50714, p. 28; `WhiteLevel` 50717, p. 29). Lens corrections are published opcodes (`OpcodeList3`, p. 58; `WarpRectilinear`, p. 106; `FixVignetteRadial`, p. 110). RW2 has fields that play similar roles, such as per-channel black levels, linearity limits and `DistortionInfo`, but their meanings are known only through reverse engineering. The DNG definitions are [V]/A and the comparison is [I]/C. Converting RW2 to DNG puts the values into documented tags, but the converter itself has to read RW2 by reverse engineering. That adds a layer of interpretation and is not an independent check. [I]

**ExifTool and LibRaw.** The two use the same tag numbers but do different jobs. ExifTool names and prints metadata. LibRaw changes values for decoding (black offsets, maximum, CFA phase). Where they disagree, record both readings. Do not pick one by preference. [D]

**Secondary descriptions.** libopenraw's RW2 page (SRC-PB007-08) gives the header as `IIU\x00\x08\x00\x00\x00` and says the CFA layout "is BGGR." The S5 samples contradict both: IFD0 is at 0x18, and CFAPattern is 1, which is RGGB in ExifTool's mapping. The page names no camera model and has no date. Treat it as D-grade background that is wrong for these samples, not as a general fact.

## Edge cases, failure modes and safeguards

- **Mode coverage.** The S5 manual describes burst and High Resolution modes; High Resolution RAW is 12000×8000 (p. 221). Panasonic documents 14-bit RAW, falling to 12-bit in burst, only for the S5II (SRC-PB007-04). Do not assume either applies to the original S5. In LibRaw 0.22.1, RawFormat 6 is decoded with 11 samples per 16-byte block when BitsPerSample (`pana_bpp`, tag 0x000a) is 14 and 14 per block when it is 12 (`decoders_libraw.cpp` lines 455–456). The data size does not choose between them: `open.cpp` lines 653–667 send both the 11-per-block and the 14-per-block size to the same `panasonicC6_load_raw` and only reject the file (`raw_count = 0`) when neither matches. [I]/C. No 12-bit or High Resolution S5 file was inspected. The burst-flagged samples are 14-bit (see above).
- **Unknown values.** If RawFormat, Compression or BitsPerSample is outside known decoder support, the result must say unsupported. If the strip size does not match the declared layout, the result must say unknown. [D]
- **Preview confusion.** The 1920×1280 JPEG is already rendered, has its own EXIF, and is much smaller than the 6000×4000 crop. It is not the primary image (MPKB-11-001).
- **Applied versus recorded corrections.** The manual's [Vignetting Comp.] setting defaults to ON for photos (pp. 354, 531) and applies to recorded pictures. Whether it changes RW2 pixel data, or is stored as a parameter, is [U]. ExifTool's PanasonicRaw table has no vignetting tag. The MakerNote tag 0x008a (`ShadingCompensation`) reads Off in all three samples. Reading it as this setting is [I]/D. `DistortionInfo` looks like parameters for a later correction, which would mean the raw data were not warped, but that reading is [I]/D and unverified.
- **Malformed input.** The 0x55 identifier and the signature do not prove the file is complete. Bound every offset. LibRaw rejects a RawFormat 6 file whose data size matches neither block size by setting `raw_count = 0` (`open.cpp` lines 653–667).
- **Private fields.** Panasonic MakerNotes and EXIF also carry personal or identifying data such as serial numbers, timestamps and owner fields. An evidence extractor must read only an allowlist of format fields.

## Panasonic LUMIX-specific notes

- [V]/A Panasonic DC-S5 Operating Instructions DVQP2213ZD: RAW pictures have the `RW2` extension (p. 479). RAW is always recorded at [L] size and 3:2 (p. 87). High Resolution mode can save RAW, and that RAW is always 12000×8000 (pp. 221–222). The firmware 2.0 supplement adds 12-bit RAW video output over HDMI (p. F-13), and the Ver. 2.3 supplement adds Blackmagic recorders (p. F-28). That output is not an RW2 file.
- [U] Neither this manual edition nor Panasonic's spec page (SRC-PB007-02) gives the bit depth, compression or container of original-S5 still RAW. The 14-bit figure here was observed in three samples (owner-reported). Panasonic does not document it.
- [V]/A Panasonic's spec page gives a full-frame CMOS sensor with a primary colour filter, 24.2 MP effective and 25.28 MP total, and an [L] 3:2 size of 6000×4000. The samples' stored raster of 6072×4016 (24.39 MP) falls between the effective and total pixel counts. That comparison is [I].
- [V]/C LibRaw 0.22.1 lists "Panasonic DC-S5" as supported (`cameralist.cpp` line 965) and has a DC-S5 colour-matrix entry (`colordata.cpp` line 1290). That is LibRaw's support claim, not a validation. The matrix values are out of scope here.

## Video/cinema extension

Does not apply to RW2. S5 RAW video leaves the camera over HDMI and is recorded on an external recorder. The manual names the Atomos Ninja V (SRC-PB007-01 p. F-13, Firmware Ver. 2.0) and the Blackmagic Video Assist 5" and 7" 12G HDR (p. F-28, Ver. 2.3). Panasonic's update notes say these record Apple ProRes RAW and Blackmagic RAW respectively (SRC-PB007-03, Ver. 2.0 and 2.3). [V]/A. It follows the recorder's format and the ProRes RAW notes in MPKB-11-001, not RW2.

## Open questions and recommended experiments

- Does any original-S5 mode write 12-bit RW2, for example burst, 6K/4K photo extraction or another mode? Needs authorized samples from each mode.
- What does a High Resolution mode RW2 contain: dimensions, RawFormat, Multishot value, CFA layout?
- How much does RawFormat 6 lose against the sensor values? Step usage was measured (owner-reported, CLM-PB007-24), but the error needs a lossless reference or Panasonic's encoder rules. One partial approach: capture well-exposed, unclipped flat-field and ramp scenes, then compare step-1 and coarser-step groups statistically.
- The crop-origin RGGB phase was observed in three samples (owner-reported, CLM-PB007-22). A colour-target capture would still give a cleaner, scene-controlled check.
- Does [Vignetting Comp.] change RW2 pixel data, or only the JPEG? All three samples have MakerNote 0x008a Off. Needs a new pair of flat-field shots, one ON and one OFF, with the same lens, aperture and exposure.
- What do `PanasonicRawVersion` 0400, the Gamma tag, tags 0x0033–0x0038 and tags 0x011a–0x0126 mean?
- Do files from firmware Ver. 2.7–2.9 differ?
